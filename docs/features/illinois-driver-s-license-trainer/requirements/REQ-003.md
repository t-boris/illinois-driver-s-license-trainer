## Statement

The application must prepare the user for the Illinois written knowledge test through a topic-based path: an illustrated lesson per topic → a quiz on that topic → a full exam simulation modelled on the real Illinois test (question count, mix of rules and signs, passing score verified against official sources). Per DEC-009, v1 topics cover every chapter of the official Illinois Rules of the Road that is examined on the written test (rules and road signs); the topic list is derived from the manual's table of contents during content preparation. The minimum question bank per topic is defined functionally: enough questions to fill that topic's share of a full exam simulation and a separate topic quiz without the two repeating the same questions; the actual numbers are fixed once the real test's question count and rules/signs mix are verified, and the bank may grow after v1.

Per DEC-001, all lessons, quiz questions, answers and explanations are authored (manually or from AI drafts) strictly on the basis of the official Illinois Rules of the Road, carry a reference to the manual section they are based on, and are manually reviewed in both Russian and English before publication. Content must be re-checked whenever the manual is updated.

Per DEC-008, all learning content is versioned in the application repository as structured files (concrete file format and schema are the implementer's choice). Each item carries metadata: the Rules of the Road section reference, the manual version/date it was verified against, and a review record for Russian and a review record for English (reviewer, date). A build-time validation step fails the build when any item lacks the section reference or either review record, so unreviewed content can never be deployed. Reviews are performed by the product owner or a reviewer designated by them, via the normal pull-request flow. Re-check after a manual update is done by comparing the stored manual version with the current one.

Per DEC-007, the app does not claim the test is English-only. It shows a short informational note (in both languages) that the written test can be taken in several languages; the details and exact list are verified on the official Illinois SOS page, recorded as an external fact before content authoring starts, and kept up to date.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] Each topic has an illustrated lesson, a quiz with an explanation for every answer option, and a visible completion state.
- [ ] The set of v1 topics covers every chapter of the official Illinois Rules of the Road that is examined on the written test, including road signs; the topic list is traceable to the manual's table of contents.
- [ ] Exam simulation draws questions across all topics, uses the real Illinois test's question count, mix of rules and signs, and pass threshold (verified against official sources and recorded as an external fact), and shows pass/fail with a per-topic breakdown.
- [ ] For every topic, the question bank contains at least enough questions to fill that topic's share of a full exam simulation plus a separate topic quiz, such that a topic quiz and an exam simulation do not repeat the same questions.
- [ ] All lessons, questions, answers and explanations are stored as structured files in the application repository and are changed only through the pull-request flow.
- [ ] Every lesson, question, answer and explanation stores a reference to the specific section of the official Illinois Rules of the Road it is based on.
- [ ] Every lesson, question, answer and explanation stores the version/date of the Illinois Rules of the Road it was verified against.
- [ ] Every lesson, question, answer and explanation stores a Russian review record and an English review record, each with reviewer and date; the reviewer is the product owner or a reviewer designated by them.
- [ ] A build-time validation step fails the build when any content item lacks the section reference, the manual version/date, the Russian review record or the English review record; such content cannot be deployed or shown to users.
- [ ] Content items whose stored manual version differs from the current official Rules of the Road version can be listed for re-check.
- [ ] The app displays, in both Russian and English, an informational note that the Illinois written test can be taken in several languages; the note's content matches the list verified on the official Illinois SOS page and recorded as an external fact before content authoring started.
- [ ] Nothing in the app's copy or explanations claims that the Illinois written test is available in English only.
